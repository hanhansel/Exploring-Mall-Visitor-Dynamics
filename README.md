Our project, Uncovering Visitor Trends: Crowd Counting in Shopping Malls, focuses on analyzing foot traffic in high-density commercial spaces to support business decision-making and improve operational efficiency. By leveraging crowd image datasets from open sources, we applied density map detection to represent and count individuals within mall environments visually. To achieve this, we trained and compared three deep learning architectures —CSRNet, MCNN, and ResNet101, across 2000 epochs each. The goal was to identify the most effective model in accurately estimating crowd numbers while minimizing overlap and error in predictions.

The evaluation was conducted using two metrics: Mean Squared Error (MSE) for accuracy of total counts and Grid Average Mean Absolute Error (GAME) for accuracy in spatial distribution. Results demonstrated that MCNN consistently outperformed CSRNet and ResNet101, achieving the lowest MSE and GAME values and showing the closest similarity to ground truth samples. While the findings highlight MCNN as the most reliable model for this dataset, limitations included the narrow scope of algorithms tested. Future work will extend the study by incorporating additional architectures, such as vision transformers, for broader comparisons and more robust performance.
